OneVest had real traction on the surface — over $20M raised during beta, 300 investors onboarded, 10,000+ people on the waitlist — but the research told a more complicated story. Investors wanted commitments backed by subject-matter experts and transparent financials; they didn't want to take anyone's word for it. And many of the projects worth funding never reached the people who would have funded them. The product worked, but the trust architecture and the discovery mechanics were both leaking value.

We rebuilt the app around four pillars: a mobile-first transition for the target demographic, a streamlined investment flow that pruned friction at every step, enhanced discoverability through curation and category, and trust-building design at every project page. We used an impact-effort matrix to keep the redesign honest about what early engineering could actually ship — pointed at the moments where small design moves had outsized effect on conversion and confidence.

What shipped was a single coherent investment surface. Project pages led with founder credentials, financial breakdowns, and risk framing — not glossy hero shots. The investment flow compressed the path from discovery to first dollar invested. Listings became browseable rather than buried. The mobile shell let us treat the experience as something users opened multiple times a week rather than a desktop session.

The redesign clarified what crowdfunding actually needs in this market: legible trust architecture and a discovery layer that surfaces the right stories to the right people. Once those two were in place, the existing demand finally had somewhere to land. The takeaway for any 0-to-1 fintech: design isn't about making the app feel exciting — it's about making the trust mechanics legible enough that users can decide for themselves.
