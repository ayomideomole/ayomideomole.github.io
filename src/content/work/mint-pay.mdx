MintPay was built for a specific user: young Africans receiving international remittances — often as pay for freelance work — and running into the same three walls each time. Extended processing times. Unclear pricing. No confidence in whether the money would actually land. The freelance economy across the continent was scaling faster than the rails behind it. We wanted to design what came next.

The first months were about narrowing scope. We talked to remittance recipients in three markets, traced the moments where trust broke down, and rebuilt the experience around them. Onboarding, account setup, and KYC verification got restructured to match how users actually moved through the product — small steps with real-time feedback, not long forms with deferred validation. Pricing surfaced before every action. Tracking surfaced after.

What shipped looked simple on the surface and was structured carefully underneath. A single payments flow with itemized fees disclosed before commit. Progressive KYC tiers so users could start small and unlock more as their needs grew. A live transfer tracker that explained where the money was at each stage instead of leaving it ambiguous. The design language stayed warm and familiar — not "fintech-cold," not novelty-driven.

Pilot retention beat regional benchmarks, and the bigger lesson was about trust. Users didn't need fewer steps; they needed visible reasoning at each step. Designing transparency into the surface — pricing before action, status after — turned out to be more valuable than any feature we could have added on top.
